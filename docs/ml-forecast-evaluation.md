## Core Contribution Statement

This machine learning forecasting sub-system provides a three-part lifecycle architecture for agricultural cost forecasting:
1. **Cold-Start Survey Prior**: Grounded in Department of Agriculture historical survey data, anchoring forecasts when zero real cycle data exists ($n=0$) and preventing catastrophic linear extrapolation errors in early cultivation.
2. **Learned Empirical Curves with Regularization**: Once real farmer cycles accumulate ($n \ge 5\text{--}10$), empirical curves fitted via isotonic regression adapt to local farm dynamics. Shrinkage with cross-validation-tuned $\kappa^*$ is **practically equivalent** to pure empirical estimation under aligned or moderately misaligned priors (S1/S2), and acts as a **critical safeguard** against defective priors (S3), automatically setting $\kappa^* \le 1.0$ (and $\kappa^* = 0.0$ for $n \ge 10$) to discard the bad prior.
3. **Finite-Sample Conformal Prediction Intervals**: Provide calibrated prediction intervals that achieve the target $80\%$ coverage rate under stationary exchangeable conditions, while transparently degrading under macro-economic or weather regime shifts (such as sudden fertilizer price spikes or delayed monsoons).

## What This Does and Does Not Show (Plain-Language Executive Summary)

### What This Benchmark Shows:
1. **Dynamic Spend Adaptation**: Cultivation expenditure follows non-linear multi-burst dynamics (split basal and top-dress fertilizer applications, mid-season weeding machinery, canal irrigation turns). Naive linear extrapolation ($\widehat{C} = S(t)/\tau$) fails severely in early-to-mid cycle (average error $> 20\%\text{--}30\%$, exceeding $40\%$ at early knots).
2. **Prior-Informed Early Advantage**: When sample size is small ($n < 10$), the Department of Agriculture historical survey prior (Method C) provides a strong inductive anchor, keeping headline trajectory errors at $13.07\%$ (S2) and $16.08\%$ (S1).
3. **Recovery from Defective Priors via CV $\kappa^*$ Tuning**: If a prior is misspecified (e.g. flat linear assumption $F(\tau) = \tau$ under S3), Method C incurs high headline error ($20.33\%$). Cross-validation selects $\kappa^* \le 1.0$ (and $\kappa^* = 0.0$ for $n \ge 10$), causing Method D to discard the defective prior and approach the empirical-only ablation ($8.16\%$).
4. **Finite-Sample Conformal Guarantees Under Exchangeability**: When calibration and test cycles come from the same macro-economic and weather regime, split conformal prediction calibrated per knot achieves the target $80\%$ empirical coverage across all knots $\tau \in \{0.1 \dots 0.9\}$.
5. **Early Overspend Alerting & Shock Sensitivity**: On cycles that breach their budget by $>10\%$, Method D detects overspending earlier than rigid heuristic rules ($+55.5$ days over production tiered rules, $+21.0$ days over tuned threshold rules) under stationary conditions. Under gradual cost inflation (+35% over 20 days), Method D detects the shock within **$1.0\text{ days}$**, compared to **$8.7\text{ days}$** for the production tiered rule ($p < 0.001$).

### What This Benchmark Does NOT Show:
1. **Does NOT prove real-world accuracy on actual Sri Lankan farms**: All evaluation is performed on synthetic daily cohorts generated by `DailySpendSimulator`. No real daily farm expenditure transaction ledger exists in the repository or from the Department of Agriculture.
2. **Does NOT guarantee interval coverage under climate or economic shocks**: Under a regime shift (+40% fertilizer price spike, delayed monsoon), the exchangeability assumption is violated, and empirical conformal coverage drops from $80\%$ down to $55\%\text{--}65\%$ in early/mid stages.
3. **Does NOT prove superiority of shrinkage over empirical estimation at scale**: For $n \ge 5\text{--}10$, the difference between Method D and pure empirical estimation with PAVA isotonic regression lies within the equivalence margin of $\pm 0.25$ percentage points; the two methods are **practically equivalent**.
4. **Does NOT guarantee early warning for unannounced mid-cycle shocks**: When overspend is caused by an unexpected crisis on days 40–80 (e.g. pest outbreak, pump failure) rather than an upfront latent inefficiency, no model can anticipate the overspend before the crisis occurs. Both Method A and Method D detect the shock immediately upon invoice entry.

## 5. Deployment Rule & Minimum Progress Policy

### Deployment Rule by Sample Size $n$
* **When $n = 0$ (Cold Start, zero closed cycles)**:
  Deploy **Method C (Survey Prior)** with split conformal prediction intervals. Minimum progress is **$\tau_{\min} = 0.30$**.
* **When $n \in [5, 9]$ (Early Learned Mode)**:
  Deploy **Method D (Shrinkage Prior with CV-tuned $\kappa^*$)**. Because empirical noise under small sample sizes elevates early-season error at $\tau=0.20$ (S2 MAPE $= 16.31\% > 15\%$), minimum progress is kept at **$\tau_{\min} = 0.30$**.
* **When $n \ge 10$ (Mature Learned Mode, $\ge 10$ closed cycles for crop)**:
  Deploy **Method D (Shrinkage Prior with CV-tuned $\kappa^*$)**. Error drops safely below $15\%$ by $\tau=0.20$ (S1 MAPE $= 13.18\%\text{--}13.82\%$, S2 MAPE $= 13.28\%\text{--}14.09\%$), allowing unlock at **$\tau_{\min} = 0.20$**.
* **Method B (Linear Extrapolation)**:
  **NEVER recommended for deployment**. Method B is never significantly better than Method D across any knot $\tau \in \{0.1 \dots 0.9\}$:
  * At $\tau=0.70$ under S1: Method B ($3.21\% \pm 0.19\%$) and Method D ($3.23\% \pm 0.20\%$) have a difference of only $0.02$ pp, lying within the $\pm 0.25$ pp margin (practically equivalent).

Therefore, under S2 Method D is statistically and practically superior to Method B ($p < 0.05$), not equivalent.
  * Everywhere else ($\tau \le 0.60$ and $\tau \ge 0.80$), Method B is significantly worse under both scenarios:
    * S1: $\tau=0.10: 73.00\% \pm 0.91\%$, $\tau=0.20: 68.87\% \pm 0.70\%$, $\tau=0.30: 46.16\% \pm 0.54\%$, $\tau=0.40: 33.94\% \pm 0.44\%$, $\tau=0.50: 23.02\% \pm 0.33\%$, $\tau=0.60: 11.96\% \pm 0.25\%$, $\tau=0.80: 7.62\% \pm 0.13\%$, $\tau=0.90: 5.81\% \pm 0.11\%$.

## 6. Shock Variants: Lump-Sum vs. Gradual Shock Delays with 95% CIs

> [!WARNING]
> **Sudden lump-sum shocks are not predicted by any method.** Instantaneous lump-sum expenses can only be registered on or after the transaction occurs.

### Variant 1: Lump-Sum Shock (+35% Instantaneous)
* **Definition**: A single emergency lump-sum expense of $+35\%$ baseline cost injected into `agrochemical` on a randomly drawn day $d^* \in [40, 80]$.
* **Mean Shock Day**: Day $61.2$
* **Detection Delays**: Method D $= 0.0$ days, Method A Tuned $= 0.0$ days, Method A Original $= 0.0$ days.
* **Paired Bootstrap 95% CIs**:
  * D vs A (Tuned): **$+0.00\text{ days } [+0.00, +0.00] \implies \textbf{equivalent}$**
  * D vs A (Original): **$+0.00\text{ days } [+0.00, +0.00] \implies \textbf{equivalent}$**
* **Why Detection Delay is 0.0 Days**: The full $+35\%$ enters the transaction ledger instantaneously on day $d^*$, immediately breaching the $+10\%$ budget threshold on that exact day. Sudden lump-sum shocks are not predicted by any method ahead of their occurrence.

### Variant 2: Gradual Shock (+35% Over 20 Days)
* **Definition**: A persistent crisis where $+35\%$ of baseline cost is distributed as an elevated daily burn rate across $20$ consecutive days: $\frac{0.35 \times \text{Baseline}}{20}$ per day from day $d^* \in [40, 80]$ through day $d^* + 19$.
* **Mean Shock Onset Day**: Day $59.5$
* **Detection Delays ($t - d^*$)**:
  * **Method D (Shrinkage Prior)**: Detected in **$1.0\text{ days}$** (Day $60.5$)
  * **Method A (Tuned Lambda)**: Detected in **$2.0\text{ days}$** (Day $61.5$)
  * **Method A (Original Tiered)**: Detected in **$8.7\text{ days}$** (Day $68.2$)
* **Paired Bootstrap 95% CIs and Effect Sizes**:
  * D vs A (Tuned): Difference is **$-0.93\text{ days } [-1.48, -0.45]$**, effect size is **$\sim 1\text{ day}$ (small)** ($\textbf{Method D detects significantly faster}$, 0 not in CI).
  * D vs A (Original): Difference is **$-7.65\text{ days } [-8.31, -7.03]$**, effect size is **$\sim 7.7\text{ days}$ (substantial lead-time gain)** ($\textbf{Method D detects significantly faster}$, 0 not in CI).
